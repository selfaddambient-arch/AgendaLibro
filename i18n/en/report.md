## 3. 一行紹介・ストア用の説明文・LP 用の説明文

### 1冊目『一人で店を回す、その横にAIが来た。』

**一行紹介**：A record of how the owner of a small basement shop in Tokyo made AI a "deputy manager" without any specialist knowledge.

**ストア用（Kindle・Google Play）**

In a basement in Higashi-Nakano, Tokyo, the author runs a shop alone: hamburger curry by day, a bar by night. This book is an honest record of how AI came to work beside them.

A long LINE message to staff, turned into a single diagram by AI, got the point across at once. A photo of a wine label became a wine list with a description and a serving price. When AI compared sales data with the menu, it pointed out that the all-you-can-drink course the owner most wanted to sell was hidden in a corner of the menu. Moving it raised the average spend per customer.

The author also built a cash-flow app and a face-recognition system by talking with AI, without writing a single line of code. When an error appeared, they didn't read it. They took a photo of the screen and showed it to the AI.

A former systems engineer, the author found that describing real problems from the shop floor matters more than technical knowledge. The steps and the failures are all here. You can start with one small thing: turning a long LINE message into a diagram.

The book is free to read and free to share.

A sequel, Uchi no mise wa, uchi no shiyō de, follows the building of a reservation system that customers' own AIs can book through.

### 2冊目『うちの店は、うちの仕様で』

**一行紹介**：The owner of a 19-seat Tokyo bar used AI to build a reservation system, then opened it so customers' own AI can make a booking.

**ストア用（Kindle・Google Play）**

Tamuro is a 19-seat bar in Higashi-Nakano, Tokyo. Its owner gave instructions to AI and built the bar's reservation system themselves. In the end they published an MCP server, so that a customer's ChatGPT or Claude can ask the bar about availability and move on to a booking.

This book records the whole process, not only what went well. It covers a discount system built at the same time as a price increase, the data showing that 88% of reservations are made on the same day, and a conversation window made for guests who had too many plans to choose from. It also tells of a mistake: an AI audit found that customers' personal information could be read from outside, and it was fixed.

The second half is about the Agenda series, tools born from one bar's problems and now shared with other restaurants. It is a small shop's account of a time when you can build what you once paid big companies for, and shape it to your own specifications.

If technical talk is not your thing, chapters 1, 3 and 5 still tell a connected story.

The book is free to read and free to redistribute. You can read it as a PDF, on note, or on Google Play.

**LP 用**
